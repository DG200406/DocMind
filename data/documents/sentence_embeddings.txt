Sentence Embeddings and the Sentence-Transformers Library

A sentence embedding is a fixed-length vector that represents the meaning of a
piece of text, such that texts with similar meaning are mapped to nearby
points in vector space. Unlike word embeddings, which assign one vector per
word regardless of context, sentence embeddings are produced by running an
entire sentence or passage through a transformer model and pooling the output
into a single vector.

The sentence-transformers library, built on top of HuggingFace's transformers
library, provides pretrained models specifically fine-tuned to produce useful
sentence embeddings. Models such as all-MiniLM-L6-v2 are popular because they
are small and fast while still producing embeddings good enough for semantic
search, clustering, and retrieval tasks. all-MiniLM-L6-v2 maps text to a
384-dimensional vector and can embed thousands of sentences per second on a
CPU.

These embeddings are typically compared using cosine similarity or Euclidean
distance. In retrieval-augmented generation, embeddings let a system find
document chunks that are semantically related to a question even when the
chunk does not share exact keywords with the question, which is the main
limitation of older keyword-based search methods like TF-IDF or BM25.

Sentence-transformers also provides cross-encoder models, which take a pair of
texts (for example a query and a candidate passage) and output a single
relevance score rather than independent embeddings. Cross-encoders are more
accurate than comparing two separately computed embeddings because they let
the model attend across both texts at once, but they are slower, since every
pair must be scored individually. This is why cross-encoders are usually used
to re-rank a small shortlist of candidates rather than to search an entire
corpus directly.
